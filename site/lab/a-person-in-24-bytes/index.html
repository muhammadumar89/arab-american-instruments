A person in 24 bytes

How a wrist band can report every five minutes for days, over links slower than old 2G.

A band on a wrist has a small battery and a long way to shout. So the message has to be tiny. Ours is 24 bytes.

FieldBytes
Band ID4
Latitude4
Longitude4
Timestamp4
Sequence number2
Skin temperature2
Heart rate1
Battery level1
Alert flags: SOS, fall, still, off wrist, battery fault1
Spare1

With wrapping and security it is about 100 bytes by the time it reaches a server. One message every five minutes means 300 people need about 0.8 kbit/s and about 9 MB a day: roughly one phone photo an hour.

Why it does not collide

GPS gives every band the exact time, so each band speaks in its own slot of the five minute cycle. Before sending it listens for a fraction of a second. The hub answers every few seconds with one short list of the bands it heard; a band that is not on the list tries again within seconds on another channel.

Nothing is dropped. A message that cannot get through stays on the band and goes out with the next one, and the sequence number puts everything back in order. SOS and falls skip the queue, go out on every path, and repeat until someone confirms.
